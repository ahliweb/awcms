feat(booking): reservation APIs with idempotency, audit, the BookingPort and the nine live events (#941, ADR-0131, ADR-0135). `booking` (still `experimental`: leaving it needs the admin screens of #942) gains `POST/GET /api/v1/booking/reservations`, `GET /reservations/{id}` and the transitions `confirm`, `cancel`, `reschedule`, `check-in`, `check-out` and the staff-only `no-show`, for slots AND nightly stays through the one allocator. Every mutation takes the shared `Idempotency-Key` (a replay returns the SAME stored response; the acting user is in the request hash; five parallel requests with one key make one reservation) and has a second idempotency layer: asking for the state a reservation is already in returns it `replayed: true` and writes nothing. A transition locks the reservation row, decides with the pure state machine on the DATABASE clock, writes exactly one history row, one audit row and one outbox event in the same transaction, and refuses as a value before any write; a confirm racing the expiry sweep has exactly one outcome and a payment observed after expiry is `409 HOLD_EXPIRED`. Reschedule is one transaction (release the old allocations, mark the old `rescheduled`, insert the replacement under the same `lineageId` with `rescheduledFromId`, move the adapter's `externalRef`), so moving over one's own slot or nights does not conflict with itself and ANY refusal rolls back to the untouched original; two reservations moving into each other's slots never hang or double-book. Opening hours, lead time and horizon are refused (`OUTSIDE_SCHEDULE`, `LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION`, `HORIZON_EXCEEDED`) unless the body asks `overridePolicy` AND the caller also holds the HIGH-RISK `booking.reservations.override`, which is checked first through the access chokepoint (action-time separation of duties applies) and audited `critical`; an override never lifts a conflict. A body naming a payment field is `400 PAYMENT_FIELD_NOT_ACCEPTED` naming it; `no-show` is marked by a person only, after the start plus a grace (default 15 minutes), and a stay cannot be checked in before its check-in date. Not-found is neutral (unknown and another tenant's id are one answer). The capability port of cross-domain-contracts section 3 is built: `_shared/ports/booking-port.ts` and its adapter (`quote`, `hold`, `confirm`, `cancel`, `reschedule`; refusals are values, idempotent by key, no override, no payment field). The nine `awcms.booking.reservation.*` events moved from the provisional AsyncAPI document to the live one and the runtime registry; they carry ids, enums, counts, UTC instants and the adapter's opaque external reference, never a customer reference, note, reason or amount, and no actor in the payload (the envelope carries `actorTenantUserId`, as other modules' events do); `expired` is published by lazy reclaim and by the sweep job alike. Decided here (booking.md section 13, left open by #939): the hold caps now DEFAULT to 5 live holds per customer and 500 per tenant (`NULL` in the settings means the module default, not unlimited), at the price of serialising the holds of one tenant on an advisory lock. Migrations `sql/208` (the `superseded_by_id` foreign key becomes DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED so a reschedule can leave the live set of the external-ref unique index before its replacement exists) and `sql/209` (`awcms_booking_reservation_event_facts`, the one allow-list of non-personal facts an event may carry, and the sweep function returning what it expired); existing tenants need nothing new (the permissions were seeded by `sql/181`). Block create/release routes are still not built (#1012).

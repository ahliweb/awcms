security(hr_payroll): the payroll catalog check moves from deny-list scanning to allow-lists (Issue #1436, ADR-0173 Proposed; direction accepted by the owner as K3). Three new rules in `payroll:catalog-boundary:check` and its daily job. (1) A non-family `SECURITY DEFINER` function whose owner can reach payroll (owner of a payroll object, the migration-ledger owner, an expected owner, a payroll grantee, an EXECUTE grantee of a family definer function, a privileged predefined role, a superuser or `BYPASSRLS` role, or a member of one) is a finding (`security-definer-not-allow-listed`) unless it is an entry of the new `PAYROLL_SECURITY_DEFINER_ALLOWLIST`, keyed `schema.name(identity args)` and pinned to its owner and body SHA-256 (`security-definer-allowlist-drift`, stale entries fail). The list is empty: every definer function in `sql/` is owned by a dedicated role without payroll privilege. (2) SQL-standard bodies (`BEGIN ATOMIC`, `RETURN expr`) are read from `pg_get_function_sqlbody` and their `pg_depend` calls count for the family-call and chain rules; an empty one is `function-body-unreadable`. (3) The required payroll tables are pinned by `PAYROLL_REQUIRED_TABLE_FINGERPRINTS` (every column and type, each required trigger with its function and `tgtype`, each foreign key with its columns): a difference is `payroll-fingerprint-drift`, and a non-family relation whose column types are within 3 of the ledger's (names ignored) is `payroll-fingerprint-match`. `PAYROLL_CATALOG_EXEMPTIONS` does not silence these codes. A migration that adds a `SECURITY DEFINER` function owned by the migration role, or changes a required payroll table without updating its fingerprint, now fails CI. No migration, API or event change.

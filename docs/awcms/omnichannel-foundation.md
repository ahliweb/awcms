# Omnichannel foundation — readiness pack (v1, 24 October 2026)

**Design only.** This pack accompanies [ADR-0148](../adr/0148-omnichannel-foundation-is-five-gated-capabilities-with-fail-closed-per-account-capability.md) (Issue #1139). It contains the logical data dictionary, the provider capability matrix, the multi-sender semantics, the threat model, the compliance reading, the contracts outline, the rollout plan and the test matrix. **Nothing here is implemented**: there is no `channel_accounts` module, no table, no route, no sender key on the WhatsApp queue and no social connector. Status words in this document are about design, never about code. Where the current code is described it is described as it is on `main`, and the documents that remain authoritative for it are the module READMEs.

Sources of the provider facts are the official documents linked from Issue #1139 and its children, as of 24 October 2026. Provider APIs, tiers and terms change; the matrix is **versioned**, every cell that is not verified is treated as denied, and any row is re-verified against the then-current official documentation by the issue that implements it.

### 2.3 Social publishing (C3, Issue #1145)

Publishing jobs reference a `channel_account` and a capability row, never a token. A job snapshots the content (the article's text and a verified media reference) at enqueue, is idempotent per `(article, trigger, account)`, goes through an approval state when the tenant requires one, and is dispatched outside any transaction. Replacing the `blog_content` no-op adapter is behind a flag, default off.

**Built by Issue #1145 (3 November 2026)** as the `social_publishing` module ([ADR-0157](../adr/0157-social-publishing-is-an-account-scoped-publication-intent-and-per-account-job-module.md), **Proposed**; [`social-publishing.md`](social-publishing.md); `sql/330` to `sql/331`): the publication, one job per account, approval, schedule, the `SocialPublishAdapter` interface with a per-provider capability map and the dispatcher. It ships with **no provider adapter**: the connectors are #1146 to #1151 and until one registers nothing is published. The worker role's secret visibility is NOT widened (`sql/328` stays WhatsApp-only): social tokens become readable only under a separate DB role brought by the first connector (ADR-0157 section 6).

### 2.4 Inbound events and contact linkage (C4, Issue #1144 decides the rest)

The event envelope is: `tenant_id`, `channel_account_id`, `provider_event_id`, `provider_conversation_id`, sender and recipient (platform-scoped ids), `privacy_class`, `occurred_at`, authenticated provenance, `correlation_id`. Idempotency key is `(channel_account_id, provider_event_id)`.

`contact_link_evidence` (only when a platform contact is linked to a CRM person): the two ids, the **method** from a closed list (`first_party_form` where the customer typed the identifier, `reply_to_addressed_message`), provenance, who and when, and revocation. No probabilistic, cross-platform or enrichment-based method exists in the list.

### 2.5 Consent

No new global consent table. Consent stays where it is per channel (`whatsapp_delivery` consent and suppression, `email` suppression and newsletter, Telegram binding) and gains the **scope** it must be keyed by: tenant, channel, purpose, and business scope where the tenant has several brands. The CRM-side consent bridge is downstream (#458).

Reading rules: a row whose label ends in `(P3)` or `(P4)` is a **phase-gated** provider (Issue #1152, §3.1): it records what the provider's official documentation was read to say on 11 November 2026, it is **not a connector**, it is not registrable, and **every cell on it is denied** (`PROVIDER_UNVERIFIED`) whatever the cell reads, until the provider's own admission ADR passes the §3.1 release gates. On those rows `restricted` names the approval, cost or policy that would have to be evidenced, and a P4 row never reads `candidate`. A provider that has no row at all is denied (`PROVIDER_UNKNOWN`). A `candidate` cell is never enough by itself: the account needs a `granted` capability row with evidence. `inbound = unverified` or `restricted` means the connector may not accept content events from that provider until a compensating mechanism is decided by ADR. Adding the phase-gated rows changed no existing cell, so the matrix stays at version 1 and no existing grant goes stale.

### 3.1 Phase-gated provider register (Issue #1152, register revision 2, 11 November 2026)

**Design and adoption decisions only.** Nothing here adds a connector, a permission or a promise. The phase-gated rows live in the §3 matrix (labels ending `(P3)` or `(P4)`) and in its code mirror, `src/modules/channel-accounts/domain/provider-matrix.ts`, where each carries a `phase`, is not registrable, declares no account kind or authentication mode, and has **every cell refused** by `cellGrantability` (`PROVIDER_UNVERIFIED`). `tests/channel-accounts-provider-matrix.test.ts` holds the two equal cell by cell. Capability stays fail-closed per account (ADR-0148 I2). ADR-0148 is Accepted (#1139); this register is the evidence base an admission ADR would start from, not that ADR.

**Verification method.** Each provider's official documentation was opened again on **11 November 2026** (revision 1 was read on 27 October 2026). A fact is written as verified only when the cited official page stated it on that date; the table below names the page and, where the page shows one, its version or last-updated date. Where a page could not be retrieved (HTTP 403, a redirect with an empty body, a fetch refused) or did not state the fact, the cell says `unverified` and the fact is **not** assumed. Third-party blogs, mirrors and community posts are not evidence and are used only to mark a point as "reported, to be confirmed". Search-engine summaries are not evidence either. The implementing issue re-verifies its row against the then-current documentation (§ intro of this pack).

**How a cell is chosen on these rows (conservative default).** `candidate` only when the official page documents the operation **and** no application review, allowlist, partnership or access cost stands between a tenant and using it. Anything that needs an approval, an allowlist, a beta, a paid credit or a quota bought by plan is `restricted`, and the restriction is named in the cell. Anything not read on an official page is `unverified`. `n/o` only where the official reference index was read and lists no such operation. On a P4 row a documented operation is `restricted (P4; …)`, never `candidate`, because P4 is feasibility only.

## 4. Multi-sender semantics (detail of ADR-0148 §4)

1. **Selection.** Explicit `senderAccountId`, else the business scope's `default_sender_account_id`, else the deployment sender. Resolution happens at enqueue and is snapshotted. If an explicit sender is given and it is not usable, the call is `refused: SENDER_UNAVAILABLE`; it does not fall to the default.
2. **Authority.** The adapter and the credential are resolved from the sender record in the authenticated tenant. A request-supplied token, phone number id or device id is never read.
3. **Affinity.** A reply to an inbound message uses the account that received it. The provider's service window is per sender and recipient; sending a reply from another number is a different conversation and is not offered.
4. **Failure.** `disabled`, `revoked`, `disconnected`, `unapproved`, `wrong tenant` or an open per-sender breaker keeps the row waiting (breaker) or fails it terminally (revoked, wrong tenant) with a sanitised reason. No other sender, number or provider is tried. Rotating numbers to evade a quality rating or a spam limit is prohibited by design.
5. **Quotas and health.** Per-sender hourly and daily caps (configurable), a tenant cap on top, throttling to the provider's documented limits, and a health state the dispatcher reads before claiming.
6. **Consent and suppression** are checked against the recipient and the **business scope**, not the sender: changing the sender does not change who consented. For WhatsApp marketing (Issue #1231 M4, owner decision 2026-11-11, option (c)) the consent stays one record per tenant and recipient, each opt-in records the business scope (brand) it was collected for, and marketing of a brand needs an opt-in for that brand (`CONSENT_SCOPE_REQUIRED` otherwise, at enqueue and at dispatch); an unscoped or pre-existing opt-in covers only marketing that names no business scope. Withdrawal and suppression stay tenant-wide. Detail: [`whatsapp-delivery` README](../../src/modules/whatsapp-delivery/README.md#marketing-consent-is-per-brand-issue-1231-m4-owner-decision-2026-11-11-option-c).
7. **Unofficial provider.** StarSender: feature flag off by default; per-operator risk acknowledgement; no claim of Meta authorisation; "accepted" is never "delivered"; status and contacts only through an authenticated path or not at all.
8. **Default per product.** A derived product may select StarSender as its default sender (owner decision, 24 October 2026) by configuring the policy, never by a code default; the other provider remains independently selectable.

## 6. Secret handling (detail of ADR-0148 §5)

- Storage is `channel_secret` rows encrypted with `src/lib/security/field-encryption.ts` (AES-256-GCM, `v2:<keyId>:<iv>:<tag>:<ciphertext>`, associated data = purpose, tenant, row, first key encrypts and all decrypt, fail-closed key handling). The ring comes from a **dedicated environment variable** for this module, `secret: true` in the config validator, outside the database and its backups. No key is needed at boot unless the module is enabled.
- Write and rotate go through one audited, high-risk, separately grantable permission with a conditional step-up (never an unconditional one, ADR-0141 §4). Reading a secret is never an API operation. A "replace token" call overwrites; there is no "show token".
- **Rotation** is "prepend a new key, run the re-encrypt job, retire the old key when no row and no restorable backup needs it". Unlike the keyed recipient hash, ciphertext can be re-encrypted.
- **Revocation** is effective on the next claim: the dispatcher re-reads account status and capability before each send, and a revoked connection clears its secret reference.
- **Telemetry:** a decrypt counter per tenant and per dispatch run, alert on an unusual rate; a startup and `security:readiness` check that the ring is present, long enough and not a placeholder.
- **Restore:** a restore without the ring must fail closed (no send, masked reads work), rehearsed in the restore drill alongside the payroll rings.
- `SecretStorePort` is the seam: the first adapter is the application ring; a KMS or vault adapter can replace it without changing callers or the stored reference.

## 10. Rollout, migration and compatibility

1. **Additive and inert.** All migrations are new files. With no account configured every deployment behaves as today, including `WHATSAPP_PROVIDER` and the single deployment credential (the `null` sender).
2. **Order.** #1140 (accounts, secrets, security review), then #1141 (sender key), then #1142 and #1143 (adapters), #1154 (admin screens) alongside; #1145 (publishing engine) after #1140, then connectors.
3. **Downstream.** `awcms-one` keeps its per-tenant `legacy`/`upstream` mode router (ADR-0047). A sender-aware port is a versioned additive change; commerce adopts it after a subtree sync as a merge commit. The StarSender default for a derived product is a configuration of the policy, applied per tenant, never a code change.
4. **No rewrite of applied migrations; no dual write; no second WhatsApp queue** during or after the change.
5. **Rollback** is forward-only: disable the provider flag or the module, keep the tables inert; the `null` sender path is always available.

## 13. Implementation reconciliation: Issue #1140 (`channel_accounts`)

Sections 1 to 12 stay the design baseline. What Issue #1140 built, and where it differs from the working names above (module [`src/modules/channel-accounts/`](../../src/modules/channel-accounts/README.md), `sql/318` to `sql/320`, [`openapi/modules/channel-accounts.openapi.yaml`](../../openapi/modules/channel-accounts.openapi.yaml)):

- **Eight tables, not seven.** The seven plus `awcms_channel_oauth_states` (the single-use `state` nonce and PKCE verifier reference of a flow in flight). The webhook-subscription table is metadata only: no route reads it yet.
- **Health is one row per account** (upsert). The bounded history is not stored: an unbounded append log would need its own purge before any consumer exists.
- **Permission names.** `AccessAction` is a closed vocabulary, so `.connect`, `.disconnect`, `.capabilities.grant` and `.assignments.manage` became `accounts.create`, `accounts.revoke`, `capabilities.configure` and `assignments.assign`; `accounts.read` and `credentials.manage` are unchanged. `senders.use` and the `whatsapp_delivery.*` sender permissions are **not** seeded: nothing enforces them until #1141.
- **Key ring and rotation (section 6).** `CHANNEL_SECRETS_ENCRYPTION_KEYS` (`config:validate` checks it shares no key with the Telegram or push ring) and `bun run channel-accounts:secrets:rekey` (manual, runs as `awcms_app`). The decrypt counter is `snapshotSecretDecryptCounts()`. The flag `CHANNEL_ACCOUNTS_ENABLED` is built, default off; every route answers `503 CHANNEL_ACCOUNTS_DISABLED` after authorization.
- **Verified callback (T1) is a verified hand-off.** The callback consumes the state and stores the authorisation code as ciphertext on a `pending` connection. The token exchange is provider I/O (I10) and belongs to the first connector's job (`activateConnectionGrant`).
- **Not built:** `ChannelAccountPort` and the `channel.account.*` events (no consumer exists; `resolveCapability` is the function the port will wrap, first consumer #1141).
- **Retention.** Each table answers the retention question in the same change through the gate-accepted forms: `subjectData` descriptors (a reasoned `NO_SUBJECT_DATA` for the health table) and a reasoned `BOUNDED_BY_DESIGN` entry per table. A delegated `data_lifecycle` descriptor for `oauth_states` was rejected: it needs a worker read grant on a control-plane table.
- **The matrix code mirror** is `src/modules/channel-accounts/domain/provider-matrix.ts`, held equal to section 3 above by `tests/channel-accounts-provider-matrix.test.ts`, cell by cell.

## 14. Implementation reconciliation: Issue #1141 (`whatsapp_delivery` sender routing)

Sections 1 to 13 stay the design baseline. What Issue #1141 built (module [`src/modules/whatsapp-delivery/`](../../src/modules/whatsapp-delivery/README.md#sender-routing-issue-1141), `sql/322` and `sql/323`, [`openapi/modules/whatsapp-delivery.openapi.yaml`](../../openapi/modules/whatsapp-delivery.openapi.yaml)), and where it differs from the working names in section 2.2 and the semantics of section 4:

- **One queue, a nullable immutable key.** `awcms_whatsapp_messages` gains `sender_account_id` (composite tenant FK, immutable by trigger, `NULL` is the deployment sender), plus `sender_selection` (`deployment`, `explicit`, `policy`, `reply`), `sender_policy_version` and the business-scope pair. A BEFORE INSERT guard refuses a sender that is not `connected`, is deleted or is scoped to another business scope. No second queue, no dual write.
- **`whatsapp_sender_policy` is `awcms_whatsapp_sender_policies`**: one row per business scope plus one tenant-wide row, `default_sender_account_id` (`NULL` is an explicit "deployment sender"), `config_version`, `effective_from`, `changed_by`. **Not built:** the optional per-purpose override.
- **`sender_risk_acknowledgement` is `awcms_channel_provider_risk_acknowledgements`** (Issue #1142, `sql/340`, owned by `channel_accounts`): provider, person, time, text version and its SHA-256, revoked by a stamp, append-only. A StarSender device cannot be `connected` without an active one (database trigger), and the send decision re-checks it. **The acknowledgement text ships as an unapproved draft**, so nothing can be acknowledged until the business owner approves it. The adapter, its gates, the status-unknown mode and what is NOT verified against the live API are in the [StarSender runbook](starsender-adapter-runbook.md) and the `whatsapp_delivery` README.
- **Per-sender health and quota persist nothing new.** Quota is counted from the queue (`sent` in the trailing hour and day, plus in flight); the breaker is the in-process per-sender `getProviderCircuitBreaker`. The dispatcher writes no `channel_account_health` row. Caps are the deployment-wide `WHATSAPP_SENDER_RATE_PER_HOUR` and `WHATSAPP_SENDER_RATE_PER_DAY`, not per-sender settings. The allowance is computed and claimed in one transaction under a per-sender advisory lock (Issue #1231 M2), so overlapping passes cannot overshoot a cap; a sender at its cap takes no fair share and no discovery slot from healthy senders (L5); a sender's verdict and decrypted credential are re-resolved every `WHATSAPP_SENDER_REVALIDATE_EVERY_ROWS` rows (default 10), which bounds a revocation window inside a pass (L1). `sql/329` makes the database guards require a WhatsApp number shape and a `connected` policy default (L6).
- **Decisions the ADR left to the implementation** (recorded here so a reviewer can disagree): (1) the capability a send needs is `dm` for transactional and security, `broadcast` for marketing and `reply` inside a thread, the matrix capabilities of that name; (2) thread affinity is the optional `inReplyToMessageId` (same tenant, same recipient), and a different explicit sender is a conflict, not an override; (3) at dispatch a sender that is gone or never approved fails the row terminally, while one that is only `degraded` or `pending`, or whose key ring is missing, is **held** (rows wait, retry budget untouched), which reads section 4.4 of this pack ("fails it terminally (revoked, wrong tenant)") and ADR-0148 section 4 together; (4) a scoped account may send only for its own business scope, exact `(type, id)` equality as in #1215.
- **Ports** (section 9): `ChannelAccountPort` and the dispatcher-only `ChannelSenderCredentialPort` (`_shared/ports/channel-account-port.ts`, capability `channel_account` 1.1.0) and `WhatsappDeliveryPort` 1.1.0 with the additive inputs. The sender-resolution adapter never imports the secret store.
- **Permissions** (section 9): `whatsapp_delivery.senders.read` and `whatsapp_delivery.sender_policy.configure` (high-risk), seeded by `sql/323` with their two routes. `channel_accounts.senders.use` and `whatsapp_delivery.risk.acknowledge` are **not** seeded: the port performs no access check, and the acknowledgement is #1142's.
- **Events** (section 9): `awcms.whatsapp.message.*` gain `senderAccountId` (absent for the deployment sender, version unchanged), and a terminal sender failure is `failureCode: sender_unavailable`.
- **Worker grants** (ADR-0148 section 5): `whatsapp:dispatch` holds column-only, read-only `SELECT` on four `channel_accounts` tables (`sql/322`) and the job-scoped `CHANNEL_SECRETS_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`; the worker also holds read-only `SELECT` on the sender-policy table because an enqueueing worker job (booking's reminder dispatch) selects the default sender, and can never write it. `awcms_channel_secrets.ciphertext` moved from the credential list to the documented worker exceptions in `scripts/security-readiness.ts`. Since `sql/324` (security audit H1) a RESTRICTIVE `SELECT` policy for `awcms_worker` limits the secret rows the worker can see to `provider_access_token`, `api_token` and `device_key`: a compromised worker cannot read refresh-token, bot-token, webhook-secret, PKCE-verifier or authorisation-code ciphertext. Purpose alone was not enough (Issue #1233 H1): those purposes also belong to Facebook, Instagram, Threads, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn and Mailketing, so `sql/328` replaces the policy with a provider-aware one: a row is visible only when a live `whatsapp_cloud` or `starsender` account's `secret_id` or a `whatsapp_cloud` connection's `access_secret_id` references it, and `security:readiness` fails if the policy is dropped or stops naming that test. It still does **not** bound the WhatsApp sender credentials themselves: the worker can read and decrypt every tenant's WhatsApp access tokens, API tokens and device keys (the key ring is in the job, and the worker chooses the tenant); this is the residual risk ADR-0148 section 5 accepts. The in-process decrypt counter is telemetry inside the same process and bounds nothing against a compromised one. The AES-GCM associated data (purpose, tenant, row) stops a ciphertext being re-labelled, and is not a mitigation against a compromised worker (the grant exposes its inputs); the comment in `sql/322` that suggests otherwise is superseded by `sql/324`'s header (an applied migration is not edited).
- **Consent is still keyed by tenant and recipient** (section 2.5 describes the scope key as future work): an opt-out and a suppression apply to every sender and every brand, the safe direction; an opt-in is not yet scoped by brand.
- **Audit hardening of #1141 (`sql/324`, same PR).** (M1) The per-sender cap now applies to every lane that is not gone at account level, including a sender approved only for `broadcast` or `reply`. (M3) A child number (a WABA phone number with no connection of its own) resolves the **parent's** connection in the sender port too, and a parent lends its connection or secret only when it is the child's container (same provider, the kind the matrix names) and `connected`; a new reason `CONNECTION_EXPIRED` makes an expired or pending grant a **hold** (like a degraded account), while a revoked or failed grant, and a revoked or disabled parent, stay terminal. (L2) A sender policy cannot be deleted (trigger), so its version never restarts. (L3) The policy-change audit row carries `previousDefaultSenderAccountId` and `previousVersion`.
- **Known follow-ups, not done here:** per-person account assignment is not applied by the port; consent is not keyed by business scope; per-purpose policy override and per-sender cap override; in-process per-sender breaker and no persisted health row; a purpose-narrower read than the three dispatch purposes (for example per sender) would need a definer function; an admin screen (#1154); a `security:readiness` check that fails when the restrictive policy is missing. Findings M2, M4, L1 and L4 to L6 of the independent audit are recorded on the PR as follow-ups.
